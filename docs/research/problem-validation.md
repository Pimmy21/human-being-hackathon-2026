# Purrpose: Problem Validation Brief

Research summary · 24 October 2026 · Compiled with Exa web research and checked against the cited sources.

## Question

Is there a real problem behind Purrpose, and is the gap it targets (checking the evidence behind **one animal's medical fundraiser**) still unaddressed?

## Verdict

**Yes, with a scope caveat.** Fake animal-rescue appeals are well documented, including in Ontario, and existing tools rate whole charities or run their own fundraising platforms. None of them checks the evidence behind a single animal's appeal across platforms. Purrpose should present itself as an **evidence review**, not a guarantee that a case is genuine.

## 1. Fraud in animal-rescue fundraising is common

- A large 2024 study of 151,966 donation-related accounts and over 3 million posts on X, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube and Telegram identified 832 scammers. Its animal-rescue cluster alone contained 125 scammers and 679 fraudulent donation posts [1].
- A six-week monitoring effort in 2024 documented 1,022 links to suspected fake rescues with 572 million combined views, and about 21% of the creators solicited donations [2].
- Fake Instagram rescue accounts have stolen real shelters' photos and videos to collect donations, diverting money from legitimate rescues [3].
- **Ontario example:** the CEO of Eastern Ontario Cats faced fraud charges after complaints about donation and expense irregularities. Ontario Provincial Police opened the investigation in October 2024 [4].
- Regulators in both Canada and the US warn that charity scammers imitate animal causes. They advise donors to check the charity (in Canada, through the CRA charity database) before giving [5][6].

## 2. Visibility favours organizations with large audiences

- Across all 704 nonprofits in Omaha Gives 2015, donor count and donation value rose with Facebook network size, posting activity and shares, after controlling for budget, age and program area [7].
- A study of US arts and culture nonprofits found Facebook fan counts associated with individual donations [8].
- In Canada, 45% of humane society and SPCA revenue comes from donations, and 85% of those donations come from individuals. Online reach varies widely across the sector [9].
- **Caveat:** these studies show a correlation between reach and donations, not that follower count causes donations. None looks at animal rescues specifically [7][8].

## 3. Existing tools leave a case-level gap

| Tool | What it does | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| Charity Navigator [10] | Rates US nonprofits at the organization level | Check a single animal's diagnosis, invoice or appeal |
| Animal Charity Evaluators [11] | Evaluates a small set of animal charities' programs | Review individual medical cases |
| Charity Intelligence Canada [12] | Rates Canadian charities' transparency and impact | Verify case-level medical claims |
| BBB Wise Giving Alliance [13] | Checks charities against 20 accountability standards | Assess individual fundraisers |
| GoFundMe Giving Guarantee [14] | Investigates problems and refunds donors after they give | Provide a public pre-donation evidence record |
| Chuffed [15], CanadaHelps [16] | Host fundraisers and donations | Establish that a diagnosis or invoice is genuine |
| Petfinder [17] | Directory of rescues and adoptable pets | Anything related to fundraiser evidence |
| **Waggle** [18] (closest analogue) | Requires vet contact and a medical estimate, reviews the case, pays the vet directly | Cover appeals hosted outside Waggle (Facebook, Instagram, GoFundMe, rescue websites) |

**The gap:** no tool reviews the evidence behind one animal's appeal wherever it is posted, explains what is supported or missing, and links each finding to its source.

## 4. What donors want to see before giving

- A redacted vet bill or estimate showing the exact need [19], plus an itemized breakdown of costs (consultation, surgery, medication, aftercare) and the expected recovery path [20].
- Regular updates, at least weekly, covering treatment, how funds were used, and photos [21].
- In a donor-trust survey, the top-rated accountability factors were independent review of how money is spent (75.9%) and truthful, non-misleading appeals (73.4%) [22].
- UK donors want to see clearly what was raised and spent, with concrete examples of outcomes, in a form that is easy to take in [23].

These match the evidence Purrpose plans to check: medical material, dated costs and funds raised, and outcome updates.

## Implications for Purrpose

1. **Lead the pitch with the Ontario fraud case [4] and the scale data [1][2].** They show the problem is real and local to the reference region.
2. **Position Purrpose against Waggle [18].** Waggle checks cases only on its own platform; Purrpose reviews the evidence wherever the appeal is posted.
3. **Phrase the popularity claim carefully.** Say "reach correlates with donations" [7][8], not "algorithms bury small rescues."
4. **Keep the "not a guarantee" framing** from the spec (R01). The evidence supports reviewing evidence, not certifying a case as genuine.
